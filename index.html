Help run the AI Factory at Cal Poly

The program

Campus-owned AI compute, built for Learn by Doing

In November 2025, Cal Poly announced the AI Factory: a $3 million investment by the Noyce School of Applied Computing in four NVIDIA DGX B200 systems, high-performance storage and networking, and the NVIDIA AI software stack. The Noyce School was created by a $60 million gift from the Robert N. Noyce Trust. It brings together Computer Science and Software Engineering with Electrical Engineering and Computer Engineering in the College of Engineering, and Statistics is an affiliate.

Students and faculty can build and train models on campus instead of renting cloud GPUs by the hour, and Cal Poly keeps ownership of the data, models and applications. Early projects cover agriculture, energy and transportation, environmental monitoring, and large-scale image and video analysis. Regional partners on the Central Coast can use it too, by working with Cal Poly faculty and students.

Faculty are already using it, and classes and research groups are lining up behind them. The three people we hire will form a new team, Academic and Research Computing, that keeps the platform running and gets it in front of the people who need it.

The team

Three roles, one small team

Each role covers a different layer. The facilitator is the front door for faculty and students. The systems administrator owns the platform. The software engineer builds the workflows that run on it. All three work closely with Noyce School faculty and with Cal Poly's existing systems and software engineering teams, so nobody is working alone.

Role 1 of 3

Research Computing Facilitator Lead

Leads the new teamFaculty-facing

You'll be the person faculty, researchers and students talk to first. You'll learn what their research needs, then point them to the right tool: the AI Factory, AWS, the National Research Platform or NSF ACCESS. About a quarter of the job is leading the team: setting priorities, handling escalations and keeping the service reliable.

What you'll do

  • Promote HPC, cloud and national research computing resources across campus
  • Consult with researchers on computational needs and recommend solutions
  • Teach the Linux command line, job submission, data management and reproducible workflows
  • Connect Cal Poly with NSF ACCESS, CaRCC, CASC and Internet2 communities
  • Analyze usage and evaluate new platforms to shape campus strategy
  • Lead the team's work planning, incident escalation and process improvement

What you bring

  • Bachelor's degree (computer science preferred) and 4 years of relevant experience; an advanced degree can substitute year for year
  • Your own research or scholarly work using computational systems
  • Knowledge of HPC, GPU and AI workloads, AWS, parallel storage and national programs
  • Comfort with Linux, Slurm, containers (Kubernetes, Docker, Apptainer)
  • The ability to explain complex topics to non-specialists and to lead others

Nice to have

  • Master's or PhD in a computationally enabled discipline
  • Carpentries (opens in a new tab) instructor certification or workshop and bootcamp experience
  • Experience helping researchers who are new to computing, in fields like the life sciences, social sciences or humanities

Tools you'll see: Open OnDemand, JupyterHub, RStudio Server, Globus, Snakemake, Nextflow, Conda, Apptainer, Git, DMPTool

Role 2 of 3

Academic and Research Systems Administrator

On-prem firstPlatform owner

You'll own day-to-day technical operations of the AI Factory and the wider research and instructional Linux environment, on campus first. That covers design, deployment, security and the steady work of keeping a shared GPU platform healthy as more people use it.

What you'll do

  • Administer the DGX B200 BasePOD: compute, GPUs, high-speed network and VAST storage
  • Run GPU-enabled Kubernetes (GPU Operator, MIG, time-slicing) and Slurm scheduling
  • Automate with Ansible and Terraform; build monitoring with Prometheus and Grafana
  • Apply CSU and Cal Poly security policy, SSO and MFA integration
  • Mentor student employees and represent Cal Poly in CSU and national forums
  • Build and operate hybrid research computing in AWS with cost and security controls

What you bring

  • Bachelor's degree in a computing field (or equivalent) and 5 years of progressive Linux administration
  • Production Kubernetes experience, including GPU scheduling and multi-tenant operations
  • The NVIDIA GPU stack (CUDA, NCCL, drivers) and InfiniBand or RoCE networking
  • Infrastructure as code, Python and Bash, Git-based CI/CD
  • Production AWS experience (IAM, VPC, EKS, ParallelCluster, FSx or equivalents)

Nice to have

  • Hands-on DGX, BasePOD or SuperPOD and Run:ai experience
  • Higher-ed research computing, NSF ACCESS, NIST 800-171 or CUI work
  • NVIDIA, AWS, CKA/CKS or RHCE certifications

Tools you'll see: RHEL, Rocky, Ubuntu, Slurm, Kubernetes, Helm, Run:ai, JupyterHub, VAST Data, Ansible, Terraform, Prometheus, Grafana, Loki, AWS

Role 3 of 3

Software Engineer, AI Systems and GPU Workloads

Software firstMulti-GPU

This role is primarily focused on the software stack and less on infrastructure. You'll write the tools and workflows that let faculty and students train, fine-tune and deploy models across many GPUs, and you'll help them make their own code faster and more reproducible. You'll work closely with the systems administrator, and the line between the two roles will blur at times.

What you'll do

  • Build and optimize workflows for training and deploying LLMs and other models on multi-GPU systems
  • Create automation for job orchestration, scheduling and utilization monitoring
  • Profile and tune distributed training with NCCL, CUDA, PyTorch and TensorFlow
  • Turn research requirements into reproducible, efficient software
  • Run consultations and training workshops on AI systems and good engineering practice
  • Validate software environments and do first-line troubleshooting with the sysadmin

What you bring

  • Bachelor's degree in a computing field and 4 years of software development including GPU-accelerated applications (or an equivalent combination)
  • Advanced Python plus C++ or CUDA
  • Distributed and multi-GPU computing and performance optimization
  • PyTorch, TensorFlow or JAX
  • Docker, Kubernetes, Slurm or Ray

Nice to have

  • Master's or PhD in a relevant field
  • Experience supporting academic research
  • Experiment tracking, CI/CD and infrastructure as code; mentoring students or junior engineers

Tools you'll see: PyTorch, CUDA, NCCL, Ray, Slurm, Kubernetes, Docker, NVIDIA AI Enterprise containers, vLLM

Why Cal Poly

A new platform and a team you get to shape

Cal Poly now has a brand-new HPC resource, and it's the most capable in the CSU. The first three people hired will enable faculty research, connect classes to advanced computing, and create the foundation for everything that comes next.

Students are part of the work here. Learn by Doing means student employees and student researchers will be working alongside you, and all three roles include mentoring them.

It's all in San Luis Obispo, on California's Central Coast, at one of the nation's premier polytechnic universities.
